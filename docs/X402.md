# Turning on pay-per-request (x402)

**Status: built, tested, and dormant.** Everything below the wallet is done. Turning it on is
setting four values; no code changes, no deploy of new logic.

This is the one thing in the agent-payment work that needs a human, because it means creating an
account that holds money. That is a CEO decision, not something to improvise inside a deploy.

---

## What is already true

`POST /v0.1/audit` is live and earning nothing yet, on purpose:

- **Free, no account, no payment:** every risk we hold about every capability a caller names —
  advisories at the version they would install today, deprecation, archived repos, single
  maintainer, registry removal — plus a `keep` / `review` / `replace` verdict. This is the part
  that must never be gated, and a test fails if it ever is.
- **Paid:** the score series behind each row and its direction of travel. Today that is reachable
  with a licence key. With a wallet it is also reachable per call.

`/api/history` and `/api/security` behave the same way, because the change went into `deny()` —
the single place every paid endpoint refuses.

Right now, every 402 quotes the subscription and the free endpoints. It carries **no** `accepts`
array and **no** `PAYMENT-REQUIRED` header, because we cannot settle a payment. Quoting a payment
option we cannot verify is worse than quoting none: the agent burns a signature and gets nothing.

---

## The decision

Three things, and only the first is really a decision:

1. **A wallet address we control.** This receives the money. I did not create one and will not —
   it is an account that holds funds.
2. **A network.** Base mainnet (`eip155:8453`) is where x402 traffic actually is. Base Sepolia
   (`eip155:84532`) is the testnet — worth one afternoon first, since a wrong `payTo` on mainnet is
   money sent somewhere else.
3. **A facilitator.** The service that verifies and settles. Coinbase runs one; the protocol lets
   anyone run one, including us, but running our own is a project and using theirs is a URL.

The asset is USDC on the chosen network — that is what the ecosystem prices in, and our amounts are
already in 6-decimal atomic units to match.

---

## Turning it on

Four secrets on the Pages project (`tashan`):

```sh
npx wrangler@3 pages secret put X402_PAY_TO      --project-name tashan   # 0x… address you control
npx wrangler@3 pages secret put X402_NETWORK     --project-name tashan   # eip155:8453  (or 84532 to test)
npx wrangler@3 pages secret put X402_ASSET       --project-name tashan   # USDC contract on that network
npx wrangler@3 pages secret put X402_FACILITATOR --project-name tashan   # https://…  (no trailing slash needed)
```

**All four or nothing.** `configured()` requires every one of them; three out of four stays dormant
rather than half-quoting a price. That is deliberate — see `functions/api/_x402.js`.

## Verifying it worked

```sh
curl -s -i -X POST https://tashan.sh/v0.1/audit \
  -H 'content-type: application/json' \
  -d '{"servers":["chrome-devtools-mcp"],"history":true}' | head -20
```

Before: `402` with `plans` and `free_result`, no `accepts`.
After: the same, **plus** `x402Version: 2`, an `accepts` array carrying your `payTo`, and a
`PAYMENT-REQUIRED` header. The free audit is still in the body either way.

Then test a real payment on Sepolia with any x402 client before pointing it at mainnet.

---

## The CDP path — payable AND findable

Settling through Coinbase's facilitator is not a vendor preference, it is the only route that is
both. Two facts decide it:

- **Every keyless option fails.** openx402 whitelists receiving addresses (`address_not_registered`);
  the other two live ones cannot parse a spec-conformant v2 request at all.
- **THE BAZAAR ONLY INDEXES WHAT THE CDP FACILITATOR SETTLES.** The Bazaar is the discovery layer
  agents search for paid endpoints, and a seller is indexed within ~30 seconds of their first
  confirmed settle. Settling anywhere else means no caller finds us — which, for a service whose
  entire distribution is being asked by an agent, is worse than not being payable.

`functions/api/_cdp.js` mints the signed JWT CDP requires: Ed25519/EdDSA, `kid` and a fresh `nonce`
in the header, `sub`/`iss: cdp`/`aud: ["cdp_service"]`/`nbf`/`exp: nbf+120` in the claims, and a
`uri` claim of `"<METHOD> <host><path>"` for the exact URL being called. 14 tests, including one
that verifies the signature against the public key — a wrong claim is a 401 with no explanation, and
a 401 means every payment is refused.

**Two more secrets, and the facilitator URL changes:**

```sh
npx wrangler@3 pages secret put X402_CDP_KEY_ID     --project-name tashan   # organizations/…/apiKeys/…
npx wrangler@3 pages secret put X402_CDP_KEY_SECRET --project-name tashan   # base64, from the same key
npx wrangler@3 pages secret put X402_FACILITATOR    --project-name tashan   # https://api.cdp.coinbase.com/platform/v2/x402
```

Both CDP values or neither: half a pair produces no Authorization header at all rather than a token
that cannot work, so a partial configuration falls back to the keyless path unchanged instead of
failing in a way that looks like a bad key.

## Choosing a facilitator — measured, not chosen from a list

`python3 pipeline/x402_probe.py <url> [<url>…]` asks a candidate three questions without deploying
it and without moving money (the probe signs `0x00`, which cannot settle): does it settle our
network, does it agree with us about the asset and EIP-712 domain, and **would it accept a payment
addressed to our wallet at all**.

Measured 16 Aug 2026 on Base mainnet:

| facilitator | verdict | why |
|---|---|---|
| `facilitator.openx402.ai` | **BLOCKED** | spec-compliant, settles eip155:8453, domain matches — but whitelists receiving addresses, and ours is not registered |
| `facilitator.xpay.sh` | **INCOMPATIBLE** | cannot parse a v2 request: "Cannot read properties of undefined (reading 'scheme')" |
| `facilitator.payai.network` | **INCOMPATIBLE** | cannot parse a v2 request: "expected object, received undefined" |
| Coinbase CDP | needs auth | answers 401 unauthenticated; `facilitate()` sends no Authorization header |

**Our request shape is the correct one**, checked against `specs/x402-specification-v2.md`:
`{x402Version, paymentPayload, paymentRequirements}`. The two INCOMPATIBLE facilitators are behind
the spec, not us — which is worth knowing, because the obvious reading of "two of three reject us"
is that we are wrong.

So openx402 stays configured and the remaining step is registering the address with it. If that
turns out to be gated behind something we do not want, the alternative is teaching `facilitate()`
to send CDP's Authorization header; the probe is how any replacement gets judged.

**A "USABLE" verdict requires a KNOWN payment-side refusal** — a bad signature, no funds. An
unrecognised answer is `UNCLEAR`, never a pass, because the first version of that classifier called
both INCOMPATIBLE facilitators usable on the grounds that their errors were not seller-side.

## What it prices

**One ladder, below** — see *The business model, with the arithmetic*. This section used to carry a
second copy of it, already missing `capability-kit` within a day of that endpoint shipping, which is
how `GROWTH.md` came to advertise a $0.20 product that never existed.

`PRICED` in `functions/api/_x402.js` is the only source of truth for a number. It holds dollars and
atomic units side by side with a test asserting they agree, and `tests/test_claims.py` fails if a
price written in this file disagrees with it.

---

## What it does not change

- **The firewall.** Nobody pays to change a score, a rank, or a listing. x402 buys access to
  measurements, exactly like a licence does, and `tests/test_firewall.py` still governs the scorer.
- **The free tier.** Every risk stays free on every surface, including this one. An audit that hides
  a known vulnerability behind a paywall is not an instrument.
- **Subscriptions.** A 402 quotes both: `plans` for people, `accepts` for software. Two ways to pay,
  not a replacement — and a *refused* licence is a 403 that is never downgraded to an x402 quote,
  because to a paying customer that reads as their subscription being ignored.

| file | what it does |

| `functions/api/_x402.test.mjs` | 39 checks, incl. every refusal path. No network, no credentials. |

Implemented against `specs/x402-specification-v2.md` and `specs/transports-v2/{http,mcp}.md` from
`github.com/coinbase/x402`, read directly. v1's `maxAmountRequired` / `X-PAYMENT` are a different
generation and a test asserts we do not emit those names.

# The business model, with the arithmetic

Prices were set by feel when the endpoints were built. This is the check.

## The settlement floor — what a call costs us to take

*Re-checked against primary sources 15 Aug 2026. The previous version of this section was wrong in
both directions and would have led to the wrong decision.*

x402's "exact" scheme has a facilitator submit a USDC transfer. **Coinbase CDP has charged since
January 2026: the first 1,000 ONCHAIN transactions a month are free, then $0.001 each.** This file
previously said "fee-free, gas sponsored — ~100% margin", which stopped being true seven months ago,
and concluded "pick a fee-free facilitator, or drop the $0.01 tier."

Both halves were wrong, and the second one matters more: **the fee is per ONCHAIN TRANSACTION, not
per payment.** CDP's docs are explicit that with `batch-settlement`, "one onchain transaction can
process thousands of payments". So the cost of a $0.01 call is not $0.001 — it is $0.001 divided
across however many payments share a settlement, which at any volume worth having is indistinguishable
from zero.

| | per-payment settlement (`exact`) | batched settlement |
|---|---|---|
| first 1,000/month | free | free |
| after that, per call | $0.001 → **10% of a $0.01 call** | ~$0.000001 → **negligible** |

We declare `exact` today (`SCHEME` in `functions/api/_x402.js`), which is correct: it is the
universal scheme and batching is a facilitator-side optimisation, not a protocol change we make.
The number to watch is onchain transactions per month, not revenue — and the first thousand are free,
so the question does not arise until there is a business.

**Nothing here argues for changing a price.** Settlement is not what constrains the cheapest tier.

Everything else is genuinely free at our volumes: Cloudflare Functions bill nothing for the first
100,000 requests a day, and the data these endpoints read is a static JSON file already on the CDN.
Marginal cost per call, absent settlement, is zero.

## Is $0.01 the right number? Measured against the market, yes — it IS the market

| service | price | what for |
|---|---|---|
| **CoinGecko** | $0.01 / request | one market-data lookup |
| **CoinMarketCap** | $0.01 / call | one API call |
| **Beacon** | $0.09 / run | one code execution |
| **tashan `capability-history`** | $0.01 | one capability's full score series |
| **tashan `config-audit`** | $0.05 | every risk in a whole config |
| **tashan `capability-kit`** | $0.25 | a pinned, verified, ready-to-run kit |

Ecosystem-wide, the median x402 payment is **$0.01–$0.10** across roughly 176 million payments, and
**76% sit below the ~$0.30 card-fee floor** — the band that exists only because this protocol does.
The average ticket is ~$0.30, which is where `capability-kit` sits.

So $0.01 is not aggressive and it is not cheap. It is the modal price for a data lookup, set by the
two most-used crypto data APIs, and a caller's agent will already have paid it elsewhere.

**And it is close to our floor anyway.** Below ~$0.001 a per-payment settlement fee would exceed the
price, so "make it cheaper" has perhaps one decimal place to run before the unit stops working. The
lever is not the price.

### The lever is the UNIT, not the price

The way to lose money here is not mispricing a call, it is selling the wrong sized thing. An agent
auditing a thirty-server config pays $0.30 and makes thirty settlements if it walks
`capability-history` row by row; the same question answered by one `config-audit` costs $0.05 and
settles once. Per-job endpoints are both cheaper for the caller and better for us, which is why the
ladder leads with them and why `/v0.1/audit` and `/v0.1/kit` take a whole config rather than a name.

### What this actually earns, stated honestly

Per-call revenue will not be the business, and the arithmetic says so plainly. Since `redact_paid()`
and the 15 Aug pricing correction, **everything about a capability's CURRENT STATE is free** — every
score, every advisory and its fixing version, the install command, the permission surface. What is
left to charge for is TIME and ASSEMBLY, and most sessions need neither: the common question is "is
this safe", which is free by design and is the distribution.

The subscription is where the money is ($6/month ≈ 600 calls; anyone needing more should be on it),
and x402's job is to be the on-ramp for a caller with one question and no account. That is worth
having and it is not a revenue plan on its own.

## Can the free tier bankrupt us? No, and here is the arithmetic

The free half of every paid endpoint is unmetered and unauthenticated on purpose — it is the
distribution — so the obvious question is what happens if an agent hammers it.

Cloudflare's published limits (checked 14 Aug 2026):

| | free plan | paid |
|---|---|---|
| Functions requests | 100,000 / day | $5/mo, 10M/mo included, then $0.30 per million |
| KV reads | 100,000 / day | $0.50 per million |
| KV writes | 1,000 / day | $5.00 per million |

At **thirty million free calls a month** — three hundred times today's traffic — the bill is about
$11. The free endpoints read a static JSON file the CDN already holds, so there is no origin cost
behind them at all.

**KV writes are the tightest limit and we are nowhere near it.** The nightly pushes 64 history and
64 security shards: 128 writes against 1,000. `history()` dedupes to one read per bucket, so an
audit of two hundred servers costs at most 64 reads. 100,000 reads a day is roughly 1,500 paid
audits — $75 of revenue a day before the read budget is touched.

**One free surface now reads KV, and it did not before.** `/api/security` was ungated (it returned
current state, which the export already publishes), so its KV read is no longer paid for by the
caller. It is one read per request against a 100,000/day budget, and the response is
`public, max-age=300`, so Cloudflare's edge absorbs repeats and only a cache miss reaches KV. That
is a real change to this arithmetic and it is written down rather than left for a bill to reveal:
if that endpoint ever became the busiest thing we serve, the read budget — not compute — is what
would bind first.

So the free tier is not a cost risk at any volume we could plausibly reach, and metering it would
buy nothing while costing the thing that makes it work. If that ever changes it will show up in the
funnel report long before it shows up on a bill.

## The ladder, and why each number

| resource | price | what it is | vs the subscription |
|---|---:|---|---|
| `capability-history` | $0.01 | one capability's score series | 600/month = $6 |
| `config-audit` | $0.05 | a whole config: what changed and which way | 120/month = $6 |
| `capability-kit` | $0.25 | a ready-to-run kit for one job, pinned and verified | 24/month = $6 |

**`security-detail` was on this ladder at $0.01 and has been removed.** It sold what we publish
free: `redact_paid()` puts advisory ids, severities, fixing versions and the install command in the
public export, and `/v0.1/lookup?name=…` returns all of them per capability with no account. The 402
quoting that price listed `/data/lookup.json` in its own `free` block as a source of the same
fields. `/api/security` is now free and ungated; its endpoint-level licence guards moved to
`functions/api/history.test.mjs`, which is the endpoint that still has a paywall.

What remains is a ladder with one rule, and it is worth stating because it is what makes it
defensible: **every priced resource sells TIME or ASSEMBLY, never the current state of anything.**
The current state is the free tier, and the free tier is the distribution.

Market anchors as of Aug 2026: CoinGecko and Circle's reference implementation both price data calls
at **$0.01**, and $0.30/call is described as competitive for a composed answer. The ladder sits
inside that.

**The crossover is the point, not an accident.** Anyone doing more than ~24 kits or ~120 audits a
month is better off on the $6 subscription, and should be — that is a human with a stack, and the
subscription is the right product for them. Per-call exists for the caller who has one question and
no account: an agent, once. The two do not compete for the same buyer, and the arithmetic says so
out loud rather than hoping nobody checks.

## Why price low when marginal cost is zero

Because the asset is coverage and the retention series, and both compound with use. An index that is
the default gets asked more questions, and every question is a signal about what to measure next
(`coverage.py` already ranks the queue by demand). Margin on a $0.25 call is not the business; being
the thing an agent asks before installing is.

## What is actually defensible

Anyone can re-wrap npm metadata, and several packages in our own corpus do. What cannot be copied
this week:

- **The retention series.** It is un-backfillable by construction — you cannot know what a score was
  in June unless you measured it in June. Every paid endpoint sells some view of it.
- **The advisory scan pinned to the installable version.** Not "does this package have a CVE" but
  "does the release you would get today", which is the question that decides an install.
- **Coverage on the demand curve.** 100% of the top 1,000 by adoption scanned, 72% graded.

## Where the demand is, measured rather than assumed

172 capabilities in our own corpus already advertise per-call payment; 144 name x402 explicitly
(`/learn/mcp-servers-that-charge-agents.html`, recounted nightly). Among them:

- **minia2a-mcp** exists to "discover and call 170+ x402 services" — a directory, and therefore a
  distribution channel the day settlement is live.
- **paygate-mcp** sells pay-per-call gating *to other MCP authors* — the sellers are tooling up.
- **pulsefeed-x402-mcp** exists to "verify x402 payment endpoints before an AI agent pays", with
  scam scoring. A market that has grown its own fraud-checking layer has fraud in it — and checking
  whether a thing is what it claims is the job this index already does.

That last one is the strongest signal in the list. The x402 economy needs a trust layer, it is
currently being built ad hoc by one of its own participants, and we measure all 172 of them already.
