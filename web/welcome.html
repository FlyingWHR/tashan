You're in.

Two steps and doctor starts telling you what to do about what it finds.

1 · Copy your licence key

Polar issued it with your subscription. Open the customer portal and sign in with the email you just paid with — Polar sends a one-time code, there is no password to make.

2 · Activate it, once

npx tashan-cli activate tashan_…
npx tashan-cli doctor

activate checks the key and stores it in ~/.config/tashan/key. That is once per machine — not once per shell, and nothing to add to your shell profile. Every doctor run after it prints Pro · licence active, so you can always see it is on.

On another machine

Run npx tashan-cli activate there with the same key. Each machine registers as its own device — named after its hostname — so the portal shows you exactly what is activated, and you can release any of them. On a machine you are done with, npx tashan-cli activate --forget hands the slot back.

Your account is at Polar

tashan.sh has no separate sign-in and looks the same whether you pay or not — there is nothing here to log into and nothing you are missing. Everything account-shaped lives in one place:

Polar is the merchant of record for the sale, which is why billing is theirs rather than ours — and why we never see or store your card.

What changed

Free doctor told you what is wrong. It still does, for everyone, forever. With the licence it also names the replacement — which one, and how it measures. Every run prints Pro · licence active, so you never have to wonder whether it took.

Nothing about your config is uploaded. The CLI reads your local files and asks us only about capability names. That was true before you paid and it is true now.
