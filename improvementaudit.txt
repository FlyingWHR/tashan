I agree. Delete “A rater that sells nothing it measures” and every derivative claim. It confuses a temporary business model with a permanent trust principle, overperforms credibility, and directly obstructs the future marketplace.
The deeper audit shows a more fundamental inversion: Tashan meticulously proves its scoring arithmetic, but barely proves its job recommendations—the actual differentiated product.
Tashan Recursive Product Audit
Executive conclusion
Tashan has the foundation of a valuable company:
A job-first directory and future marketplace for AI skills, supported by independent evidence about fit, maintenance, compatibility, adoption, and known risk.
The current site, however, is still designed like a measurement project trying to establish moral authority, rather than a high-quality product that naturally earns confidence.
Its credibility problem is not that users have insufficient explanations. It is that several visible product outputs contradict one another:
	•	Job rankings frequently surface capabilities that are only tangentially related to the job.
	•	The methodology extensively explains the score but does not explain how skills are assigned to jobs.
	•	Different pages show different scores and paid-feature descriptions for the same capability.
	•	Deprecated or nonfunctional products can remain “active,” ranked, and installable.
	•	Compatibility appears broadly inferred rather than specifically verified.
	•	Security language sometimes converts missing detection into reassuring presentation.
	•	“Best,” “trusted,” and “expertise” are used more strongly than the underlying measurements justify.
The correct response is not another round of disclaimer writing. It is to simplify the claims and strengthen the product system.

1. The central strategic correction
Current implicit model
The site currently behaves as though its primary product is:
A defensible score applied to a large index of AI capabilities.
Job and role pages are then generated by assigning capabilities to tasks and sorting them using that global score.
Correct model
The primary product should be:
A system that helps users choose the right AI skill for a specific job.
The measurement system is supporting infrastructure.
That changes the hierarchy:
	1.	What is the user trying to accomplish?
	2.	Which skills directly fit that task?
	3.	Which of those are compatible with the user’s environment?
	4.	What evidence supports each option?
	5.	What are the trade-offs?
	6.	Which one should the user install?
	7.	Has anything materially changed after installation?
At present, Tashan explains questions four and partly seven while insufficiently solving questions two, three, five, and six.

2. Delete the independence performance
Delete this positioning
A rater that sells nothing it measures.
The About page builds the company identity around not selling, hosting, or profiting from the measured products. Pricing repeats that Tashan “sells nothing we measure, hosts nothing, and takes no cut,” and presents this as the only reason its verdict is valuable. Similar claims appear in the English and Chinese learning content. (Tashan⁠)
This framing is wrong for four reasons.
It locks the business model
A future marketplace necessarily facilitates transactions and takes revenue associated with products Tashan evaluates. The current copy makes that evolution look like a betrayal.
It does not prove independence
A company can avoid transaction revenue and still produce flawed, biased, or careless rankings. Conversely, a marketplace can maintain credible separation between commercial activity and evaluation.
It overstates the competitive argument
Current competitors already combine discovery with security scoring, verification, hosting, deployment, and commerce. Glama explicitly offers verification, rebuilding, scoring, testing, and deployment; Smithery combines registry, gateway, and commerce infrastructure. Tashan cannot differentiate merely by refusing to monetize the supply side. (Smithery⁠)
It makes the brand defensive
Repeated statements about sponsorships, bought rankings, payment firewalls, re-derivability, and impartiality make the site sound like it expects distrust.
Replace it with a permanent governance principle
Commercial relationships never influence task fit, measurements, rankings, findings, or editorial recommendations.
This survives every future business model.
Tashan may eventually:
	•	Sell paid skills
	•	Take transaction fees
	•	Charge publishers for analytics
	•	Charge for verification workflows
	•	Sell team governance
	•	Sell API access
But publishers must never be able to purchase:
	•	A higher rank
	•	A stronger fit label
	•	A higher score
	•	Removal of a finding
	•	A positive review
	•	Inclusion in a recommendation
	•	Preferential comparison treatment
This is a product governance rule, not a company purity narrative.

5. Current job ranking logic undermines the positioning
The task pages state that capabilities are associated with the work and then ranked by the global Tashan score, which measures upkeep, freshness, and adoption—not task performance. (Tashan⁠)
This produces visibly weak recommendations:
	•	The top product-management result is Impeccable, a frontend design plugin.
	•	Mattpocock Skills is second for product managers because it touches project-management workflows and has enormous repository adoption.
	•	Architecture Studio, built for architects and AEC work, is ranked first for legal and compliance.
	•	Revolut X, a cryptocurrency trading tool, is ranked first for financial modelling.
	•	Bettercallclaude is ranked first for contract review despite its own documentation grade being “thin.” (Tashan⁠)
These are not isolated bad rows. They reveal the ranking architecture:
Broad task tagging + global popularity/maintenance ordering ≠ job recommendation.
Correct ranking architecture
Use a two-stage system.
Stage 1: Eligibility and fit
A capability must pass:
	1.	Direct or meaningful task fit
	2.	Relevant jurisdiction, platform, or domain scope
	3.	Compatible installation path
	4.	Functional and nondeprecated status
	5.	Sufficient evidence to support the assignment
Stage 2: Ordering within the eligible set
Rank or filter using:
	1.	Task fit
	2.	Instruction or implementation quality
	3.	Operational health
	4.	Adoption
	5.	Known-risk status
	6.	User-specific compatibility
The global Tashan score can remain one input. It cannot be the ordering logic for job pages.

19. Build-system and data-quality requirements
The visible contradictions indicate separate templates or data snapshots are being generated without sufficient cross-page validation.
Mandatory consistency tests
A deployment should fail when:
	•	A score differs between dossier, category, role, task, comparison, API, and llms.txt.

	•	A security check presents absent detection as absent access.
	•	A task page uses “best” without task-performance evidence.
	•	A capability’s task assignment lacks a source.
	•	An install command fails syntax validation.
One snapshot identifier
Every generated page should use one immutable build snapshot:

All pages, exports, and comparisons should be produced atomically from that snapshot.
Field-level provenance
Every important field should internally store:

	•	Source
	•	Method
	•	Confidence
	•	Last checked
	•	Override history
